Case Study — Factory OS

Factory OS — app design, organised and documented

Factory OS connects garment and footwear manufacturers with the brands that buy from them. The design covers both sides: a Partner App where a factory sets up its profile, tracks inventory and production work, and manages staff; and a Marketplace App where a brand discovers manufacturers, negotiates price, and places wholesale orders. The file is laid out so someone with no design background can follow it — every screen has a code and a one-line explanation.

What the design covers

  • Partner App: onboarding, a four-step factory setup, home, inventory, workflow, staff and roles, chat, notifications
  • Marketplace App: sign-up, discover and search, manufacturer profile, product, cart and checkout, orders
  • A design-review page: screenshots of the built app with the fixes needed, and the screens redrawn in response

How the file is organised

  • Pages named for their audience; sections ordered the way a user meets them
  • Screen codes such as P7.12 or M6.08, so a screen can be referred to without ambiguity
  • A “start here” guide with a glossary (OTP, MOQ, GST, empty state) and a list of open questions

Unified Expenses

Independent product case study — expense management for a mid-size enterprise

of expenses mismatched at Finance
$10–20
small expenses employees don't bother filing

The trigger: three different people were frustrated by the same expense system, for what looked like three different reasons. Managers only saw spend a month after it happened. Finance manually corrected roughly one in five submissions. Employees quietly stopped filing anything under $20 because it wasn't worth the hassle. Tracing all three back, the root cause was the same — the system only learns about spend when someone remembers to type it in, days after the fact.

The redesign

  • Multi-source capture — expenses are created from receipts, bookings, and card feeds, not just manual entry
  • A confidence score per field, so mismatches surface as a judgment call instead of a silent guess
  • Conflict reconciliation that routes disagreements to a person instead of auto-rejecting
  • Expenses grouped by trip, so a manager reviews one summary instead of five stray receipts

What I held firm on

  • A person always makes the final approval call — never a fully automated decision on someone's money
  • North star metric: straight-through rate (clean, zero-correction approvals) instead of raw speed, so the number can't be gamed by rubber-stamping
  • An explicit MVP cutline — Phase 1 proves the model works end to end; automatic receipt-reading and fraud detection wait for Phase 2

Delivered as a 17-slide product spec plus three clickable prototypes — employee filing flow, manager approval flow, and a finance rules console — built to walk a panel through the reasoning end to end, not just the screens.
